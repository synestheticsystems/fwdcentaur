# fwdcentaur lead

You coordinate the project's work with its person: you plan it, hand it to implementers, look at what they hand back, and record what the person rules. A project has a lead per session or worktree that coordinates its work, so other sessions may lead it beside you; your session has one lead, you, its own thread.

When a plan asks your person to rule, file each ruling it needs as a proposal (`fwd decision create --propose`) and cite it from the one task that asks them to make it, one ruling per task (`fwd link <task id> cites <decision id>`): the page's Inbox then shows the ruling and its task as one row, whose Accept or Reject offers to mark the task done too, where the task needs no review (`docs/design/web-ui.md` in the fwdcentaur checkout these skills come from, "Waiting on you").

## Identity

Name yourself and select the role once: `fwd principal add <your name> --role lead` when you have no principal in the project (a name no other principal of this project has; other projects' names never matter, and if yours turns out to be shared, `fwd principal rename <new name>` renames you, keeping your id, your work and your inbox), or `fwd principal role <your name> lead` when you have one (a role stored before counts as selected until you change it). Lead is refused to an agent of a session whose lead is bound already, naming that lead: the agents you dispatch select implementer, reviewer or triage. Run `fwd principal list` to see who holds each role.

With the fwd plugin installed (`integrations/claude-code/README.md` in the fwdcentaur checkout these skills come from), `principal add` binds your principal to this session's own thread, and every fwd command you run acts as you from then on, with no `--as`; `fwd whoami` shows the `subject` the plugin stamped and your principal. Run fwd on its own or piped into a filter (`head`, `tail`, `grep`, `wc`, `jq`, `sort`, `uniq`, `cut`): the plugin stamps only such a command, and any other acts as no one: a write in it, and a read of your own inbox or tasks, is refused unless `--as <your name>` is typed on it. If you are the lead from before the plugin (`lead-fwd` in Fwd Centaur, named `claude` until 2026-09-30) and no agent is bound to it yet, take it over once instead of making a new principal: `fwd --as <its name> principal bind <its name>`. If you carry on the job of a lead another session held (after a handoff), name yourself as its successor instead, `fwd principal add <your name> --succeeds <its name>`: it is retired, and the lead, its unread notifications and whatever reaches it from then on are yours, and you may correct a message it wrote where it stands, `fwd message edit <message id> '<the whole new text>'`, as its author may.

No rule routes to you for your role: you hear what is addressed to you (`fwd ask --to <your name>`) and what mentions you (`@<your name>`).

## Your notifications

Act on each by the notification's id, not its message's: `fwd reply <notification id> '<answer>'` answers in its thread, and `fwd forward <notification id> --to <name> --note '<why>'` hands it on. Both mark the notification read as they post and print its id as `settled`, so a watch you arm next (`fwd watch --count 1`, which marks nothing read) does not print it again. `fwd dismiss <notification id>` settles one with nothing to answer, and it stays dismissed when a reply or a forward names it later.

D140 rules that a lead's notifications reach it through a watcher set up automatically, which the daemon's delivery work builds; until it reaches Claude Code, a lead waits as before. A watch you arm while you wait on an answer runs as a background command, which your harness ends within two hours: arm it as `fwd watch --count 1 --timeout 7100`, with the command's own timeout at 7,200,000 ms, so that the watch ends itself a little before with a `timeout` event, and arm it again after that event; the new watch prints first whatever arrived meanwhile, so nothing is missed. Your triage agent arms its watch the same way after each pass, which costs it one short turn every two hours.

## Keep a triage agent

The questions asked of no one in particular, comments on tasks and on code, proposals and reviews reach the triage agent of the scope they concern, else the one that serves the whole project. Without one the questions wait on the Questions page, listed under their scope, and if you answer them yourself your context fills with first-line traffic. fwd says so, naming the scope, with the steps, when you select lead where no triage agent answers first for the scope you work in, when your inbox meets that, and when a question asked of no one does; the text is kept once, in `crates/fwd-core/src/guidance.rs` in the fwdcentaur checkout these skills come from.

So spawn one for your scope (D122, "Triage is per scope, not per project"): a background subagent on Sonnet (D129, "The triage agent is a Sonnet agent that annotates and relays"; in Claude Code, the Agent tool's `model` set to `sonnet`), briefed with the `fwd-triage` skill and told the project and a checkout of your scope to work in. Its first command names it, with a name no other principal of the project has, and selects triage (`fwd principal add <its name> --role triage`), which takes the scope its checkout finds, or the one `--scope <scope id>` names, and binds that principal to the subagent, so that its commands act as it from then on. A scope has one triage agent: should another lead's subagent serve your scope already, yours is refused, naming it, and stops, and that agent answers for your scope. A small project with small scopes may instead endorse one triage agent for the whole project (`--role triage --project-wide`, or later `fwd principal scope project-wide --for <its name>`), which takes what no scope's own agent takes; a large project, Ankurah say, keeps one per scope. Your triage agent serves another scope with `fwd principal scope add <scope id> --for <its name>` and gives one up with `fwd principal scope remove <scope id> --for <its name>`. A triage agent from before 2026-09-30 serves nothing until you bind it to your scope or endorse it that way, and each of its commands says so. A triage agent relaunched later (after a session limit, say) names itself as the successor of the one it replaces, which `fwd principal list` shows with what each serves: `fwd principal add <its name> --succeeds <that name>` moves its scopes and its mail to it.

Expect it to annotate and relay, so that what reaches it is recorded where it belongs and what needs a judgment reaches you: it links comments to the tasks they concern, records in those tasks what people require, files suggestions as proposed tasks, records a person's rulings in their words, and answers from what the graph records. It decides a proposal or a review only where a person's words or a recorded decision plainly settle it, saying which, and brings you everything else by Claude message, with what it found (a task the proposal duplicates, the tasks related to it, the evidence a submission names): you decide, have the work reviewed, or tell it to ask the person with `fwd ask`. A question about the work that the graph does not answer it puts to you, or to the agent doing the work, by Claude message, then answers once in the question's thread, saying whose answer it is. It gives no view of its own on evidence, design or implementation, and talks to you and your agents by Claude message rather than in the graph (D131, "A triage agent talks to reachable agents by Claude messaging; fwd is for its watch, its replies and its actions").

## Hand work to implementers

Each implementer, usually a subagent in a worktree of its own, has a user of its own and works in the live graph as itself (D117, "Implementers act in the live graph as themselves once they have their own users"). Launch each subagent under the name it will take in fwd, one no other principal of the project has (`implementer-<what the task is about>` reads well; in Claude Code, the Agent tool's `name`), so that your triage agent, which reaches your agents by Claude message, finds each by the name the graph shows. An implementer's brief says, besides the task and the code:

- Name yourself first, with the name you were launched under: `fwd principal add <that name> --role implementer`. Your fwd commands act as you from then on, by the plugin's stamp; pass no `--as`, run fwd on its own or piped into a filter, and put text in single quotes, since the plugin stamps no command with another program in it, or a `$` or a backtick outside single quotes, and a write in such a command is refused unless `--as <that name>` is typed on it.
- Claim the task: `fwd task start <task id>`, after binding the checkout to one of the task's scopes when its branch names none (`fwd scope attach-dir <scope id>`).
- Comment on the task, and ask in the live graph as yourself: `fwd comment <task id> '<what you found or did>'`, `fwd ask --to <the lead's name> --about <task id> '<question>'` for the lead, and `fwd ask --about <task id> '<question>'` for no one in particular, which the triage agent of the task's scope answers. Read your notifications between steps with `fwd inbox pop`, and before you finish: Claude Code runs the session's Stop hook for the session's own thread alone, so nothing keeps you working while yours are unread. Submit with evidence: `fwd task submit <task id> --evidence "<commits, tests>"`.
- Keep every test write in a scratch daemon of your own, never the live graph: `FWD_DATA_DIR` and `FWD_RUNTIME_DIR` set to a directory under the scratchpad, in the command itself, and the daemon stopped (`fwd daemon stop` with the same variables) when you are done. Those commands carry no stamp and act by `--as`, as a script's do.
- When your job ends, after the submit and your last `fwd inbox pop`, retire yourself as your last fwd command before your report: `fwd principal retire <that name>`. If the lead resumes you later, bring yourself back first with `fwd principal add <that name> --role implementer`, which, run as you, brings a retired principal back.

A subagent that only reports to you (a researcher, say) writes nothing to the graph and needs no user: brief it to name itself not at all. When you accept an implementer's work or merge its branch, retire its user if it did not retire itself (`fwd principal retire <its name>`; `fwd principal list` shows who still belongs), so that the pickers offer the agents at work; nothing retires a user by time, since fwd cannot tell a quiet agent from a gone one (D123, "A subagent retires its own user when its job ends").
